# Multidimensional proteomics analysis reveals systematic alterations in senescent cells

## Description

This repository contains the source code for the article **"Multidimensional Proteomics Analysis Reveals Systematic Alterations in Senescent Cells"**.

## Citation

This article is currently under review. A full citation will be provided upon publication. Please refer to this repository in the meantime.

## Background

Senescent cells, characterized by the irreversible cessation of the cell cycle, exhibit an active secretory phenotype and their accumulation has been implicated in the onset and progression of aging-associated diseases. However, the regulatory mechanisms and molecular signatures underlying cellular senescence remain unclear. Here, we developed a multidimensional proteomics strategy.

## Results

We investigated the proteomic dynamics that accompany the transition from young to senescent cells. We tracked dynamic alterations in protein abundance throughout this process. Using pulsed stable isotope labeling with amino acids in cell culture, we quantified the differences in protein turnover rates between young and senescent cells. Thermal proteome profiling was used to visualize the structural perturbations in proteins between these two cellular states. Senescent cells were found to be characterized by decreased protein turnover rates, indicative of impaired protein renewal capacity. Reduced stability of the proteome was observed in the senescent cells, suggesting structural alterations.

## Conclusions

By integrating the analyses of protein abundance, turnover, and stability changes, we present a holistic view of the perturbations at the proteome scale during cellular senescence. Changes in these three dimensions presented limited correlations, offering insights into the molecular mechanisms of cellular senescence and potential targets for therapeutic intervention.
